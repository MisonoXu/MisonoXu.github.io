Our group develops control-theoretic methods for networked multi-agent systems in artificial intelligence and embodied intelligence. We study how collective intelligence forms, how it is implemented through networked feedback, and how systems reconfigure in open environments. A central question is how agents with only local information can achieve stable, fast, and optimal coordination when communication networks and physical dynamics are uncertain or constrained.

<section class="research-section" id="delay">

<span class="eyebrow">Research 01</span>

## Delay-Tolerant Distributed Optimization and Control

We study distributed optimization and control of nonlinear networked systems with input delays and communication constraints. Using only local and neighboring information, our methods seek stable coordination and convergence to a global optimum within the admissible delay range.

<span class="eyebrow">Research 02</span>

## Dynamic Games and Resilient Reconfiguration

We investigate rapid game-equilibrium seeking and task reconfiguration in open multi-agent systems. The research addresses agents joining or leaving, switching network topologies, complex constraints, and node failures, with the aim of updating collective decisions while maintaining constraints and restoring system resilience.

<span class="eyebrow">Research 03</span>

## Human and AI in the Loop for Multi-Robot Systems

We study teleoperation, feedback estimation, and cooperative control for networked multi-robot systems driven by human or AI decisions. Under limited communication and uncertain dynamics, the goal is to translate high-level intent into stable, real-time, and reliable closed-loop execution by robot teams.
